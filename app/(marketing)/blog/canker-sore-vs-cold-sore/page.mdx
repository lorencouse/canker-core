Three questions settle this, and you can answer all three in front of a mirror
in about ten seconds.

1. **Which side of the lip border is it on?** Inside, on the wet lining — inner
   lip, cheek, tongue, floor of the mouth — says canker sore. On the border
   itself, or on the dry skin outside it, says cold sore.
2. **Did it blister first?** A cold sore starts as small fluid-filled blisters
   that break and crust. A canker sore never blisters; it opens straight into a
   shallow crater.
3. **Is it one, or several together?** Canker sores usually arrive singly. Cold
   sores come as a cluster in one patch, at slightly different stages.

## What is the difference between a canker sore and a cold sore?

| &nbsp;         | Canker sore                         | Cold sore                            |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Where          | Inside the mouth, on movable lining | Lip border, or skin just outside it  |
| Starts as      | An open crater                      | A cluster of blisters                |
| Looks like     | White or yellow floor, red ring     | Blisters, then weeping, then a scab  |
| How many       | Usually one                         | Several together in one patch        |
| Cause          | Not a virus                         | Herpes simplex, usually type 1       |
| Contagious     | No                                  | Yes, most while blistered or weeping |
| Comes back     | Anywhere, or the same spot          | Nearly always the same spot          |
| Typical course | 7 to 14 days                        | 7 to 10 days                         |

Two rows do all the work: **where** it is and whether it is **contagious**.
Those are facts about two different conditions rather than judgements about
your sore, which is what makes them worth having. Nearly every other
difference — how much it hurts, how big it gets, whether it woke you up —
overlaps enough between the two to be useless.

## Canker sore on the lip, or cold sore?

This is the case that sends people looking, because the lip is the one place
both are plausible, and the answer is a line rather than a feature.

The **vermilion border** is the edge where the coloured part of your lip meets
the skin of your face. Run a fingertip over it: the lip side is smooth and
slightly damp, the outside is ordinary dry skin.

- **Inside the lip**, against the wet lining, where it catches on your teeth:
  canker sore.
- **On the border, or outside it** on dry skin: cold sore. A canker sore
  essentially never appears on dry skin, because the tissue it forms in is not
  there.

If it is genuinely sitting on the line and you cannot call it, the sequence
decides instead: wait a day. Blisters mean cold sore. A crater that was never a
blister means canker sore.

## What about a fever blister?

Fever blister, cold sore and herpes labialis are three names for one thing. The
comparison people usually search for — canker sore, cold sore and fever blister
— is really a comparison of two conditions with three names, and the canker
sore is the only one that is a separate thing.

## Is a canker sore herpes?

No. Canker sores are not caused by a virus, are not contagious, and have
nothing to do with herpes simplex. That question deserves more than a
paragraph, so it has [its own page](/blog/are-canker-sores-herpes) — including
the part most comparisons skip, which is that a first herpes infection genuinely
can cause ulcers inside the mouth, and what tells that apart from an ordinary
canker sore.

## How long does each one last?

A cold sore runs about 7 to 10 days: tingle, blisters within a day, weeping,
crust, then skin. A canker sore takes [7 to 14
days](/blog/how-long-do-canker-sores-last), and a big one longer.

Both share the trap that makes people think theirs is going wrong: the pain
peaks early and eases while the sore is still at its widest, so by the middle of
the week it hurts less and looks worse. That is the normal shape of both, not a
sign that something has gone wrong.

## So it is a canker sore. What now?

Then you know two useful things: it is nobody else's business — there is nothing
to catch or pass on — and it has a course, which means today's appearance is not
information on its own. One look tells you nothing; the same sore measured twice
tells you everything, and the gap between those two facts is the whole reason
people spend a fortnight wondering whether theirs is stuck.

The [five stages](/blog/canker-sore-stages) are what to expect. [Whether yours
is healing](/blog/is-my-canker-sore-healing) is a question about millimetres,
and it takes two measurements two days apart to answer.
