Most pages about this show you photographs of other people's mouths, which
tell you very little: a photo has no scale, no date, and no way of saying
whether the sore in it was getting better or worse that morning. What follows
is drawn instead, all five stages at one millimetre scale, so the sizes can be
compared to each other and to yours.

These are the stages of a canker sore specifically. A cold sore runs through a
different sequence — blisters first, then a crust — and [the two are drawn side
by side here](/blog/canker-sore-vs-cold-sore) if you are not yet sure which one
you are looking at.

## What are the stages of a canker sore?

### 1. Prodrome — day −2 to 0

The stage that is felt rather than seen. A tingle, a prickle, or a spot on
your lip that feels tight when you move it. There may be nothing visible at
all, or a small pale bump a few hours before it opens.

Almost nobody logs this one, and it is the most useful one to log. If you get
sores often, the tingle is the only advance warning you will ever get, and
knowing that yours reliably arrives two days ahead turns a surprise into a
pattern.

### 2. Ulceration — day 1 to 3

The crater opens. A shallow round ulcer with a white or yellow floor inside a
bright red ring, and it widens every day of this stage.

This is also when it hurts most, which matters more than it sounds: **the pain
peaks before the size does.** Salt, citrus, toothpaste and the corner of a
crisp all find it.

### 3. Peak — day 4 to 6

At its widest, and holding still. The ring is still red but no longer
spreading, and the pain has already started to ease.

This is the confusing stage, and it is where most people decide something is
wrong. The sore looks worse than it did on day two — it is genuinely bigger —
while hurting less, and nothing changes for two or three days running. That
plateau is the normal middle of a normal sore, not a stall.

### 4. Granulation — day 7 to 10

Healing, visibly. A canker sore closes from the edges inward, so the white
centre shrinks while the overall spot is still there. A sore that is mostly
pink with a small white middle is further along than one that is mostly white,
even at the same width.

### 5. Healed — day 10 to 14

Closed. A faint pale patch may sit there for a few days afterwards. Minor
canker sores do not scar; if something is still marked in that spot weeks
later, it is worth showing to a dentist, because it is not the sore.

## How long does each stage last?

| Stage       | Days     | Typical width | What changes            |
| ----------- | -------- | ------------- | ----------------------- |
| Prodrome    | −2 to 0  | no ulcer      | you feel it             |
| Ulceration  | 1 to 3   | 2–5mm         | opens, widens, hurts    |
| Peak        | 4 to 6   | 4–8mm         | nothing — it holds      |
| Granulation | 7 to 10  | 2–5mm         | fills in from the edges |
| Healed      | 10 to 14 | closed        | pale patch, then gone   |

These are the ranges for a minor aphthous ulcer, the ordinary kind under about
10mm. Larger ones stretch every stage and can run for weeks. If you want the
arithmetic for a particular sore, the [duration
estimator](/blog/how-long-do-canker-sores-last) does it by size.

## Does a canker sore turn white when it heals?

No, and this is the most common misreading of the stages. The white or yellow
floor is fibrin — the same stuff as a scab, minus the drying out — and it is
there from the moment the ulcer opens. It is not new skin, and its appearance
is not a milestone.

What it does is get **smaller**. The useful question is not "is it white" but
"is there less white than there was", which is a comparison, and comparisons
need two observations.

## Which stage is this one in?

The stages are easy to tell apart on a diagram and hard to tell apart in a
mirror, because what separates stage 3 from stage 4 is a change in width of a
millimetre or two — and nobody can hold a millimetre in their head from
Tuesday.

The shortcut is that you do not actually need to name the stage. Two
measurements two days apart answer the only question the stage was standing in
for: [is it getting smaller](/blog/is-my-canker-sore-healing). Widening means
stage 2, static means stage 3, narrowing means stage 4. The number tells you
the stage, not the other way around.
