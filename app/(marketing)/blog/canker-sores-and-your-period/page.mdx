Most trigger questions are hard because the candidate is vague and its timing
is unrecorded. This one is the exception, and it is worth saying plainly: your
cycle is the only trigger on the list that comes with dates already attached.

That makes it the one question in this whole subject that a few months of
logging can genuinely settle.

## Can your period cause canker sores?

For a minority of people who get recurrent canker sores, they arrive in a
consistent relationship to the cycle — most often in the week before a period.
For most, they do not, and the ulcers turn up scattered across the month with
no relation to it at all.

Both of those are ordinary. What no article can tell you is which one you are,
and the general finding is a poor guide: hearing that hormones are a recognised
trigger will make you notice the sores that fit the story and forget the ones
that did not.

## Where in the cycle, if there is a pattern at all

Where a pattern exists, it tends to sit in the **luteal phase** — the stretch
between ovulation and the start of bleeding — with the sore appearing in the
few days before the period rather than during it.

But the phase matters much less than the **consistency**. A sore in the week
before your period three cycles running is a pattern. One in that week, one
mid-cycle and one during a period is not a pattern with an exception in it; it
is an absence of pattern, and reading it as the former is how a coincidence
becomes a belief.

## How to tell whether yours follow your cycle

Two dates per event, and arithmetic:

1. **Record the first day of each period.**
2. **Record the day each sore first appeared** — the day you first felt it,
   not the day it looked bad.
3. **Count the days between the last period start and the sore.** That number
   is the sore's cycle day.
4. **Read the numbers after three or four cycles.** Cycle days landing within
   a few days of each other every time is a real pattern. Spread across the
   month, it is not, and no amount of further waiting will condense them.

Three cycles is roughly three months, which is the honest cost. It is also the
shortest version of this that exists, because a cycle is the unit and you
cannot get four of them any faster.

## The confound worth naming

The premenstrual week is not only a hormonal event. For a lot of people it
also carries worse sleep, more stress, different eating and sometimes a
bitten cheek from a clenched jaw — which are four other entries on the same
trigger list, all arriving in the same window.

So a sore that lands reliably in that week has genuinely told you _when_,
and has told you nothing about _which_. Separating them needs the other
triggers logged as well, so that the premenstrual weeks where you slept fine
can be compared against the ones where you did not. That is a subtler question
than most people need answered — but it is the difference between "my cycle"
and "the week my cycle makes hard", and only one of those has anything to do
with hormones.

The same base-rate trap applies here as everywhere else, and [the stress
page](/blog/stress-and-canker-sores) is where that arithmetic is worked
through: a trigger that is present most of the time explains nothing, however
often it precedes a sore.

## Contraceptives, and the one clean before-and-after

Reports of hormonal contraception changing canker sores go in both directions
and there is no reliable general answer, which is exactly what makes a start
or stop date one of the most informative things a log can contain.

It is the closest this subject comes to an experiment: a clear date, with
months of ordinary record either side of it. If your rate changes noticeably a
couple of months after that date, that is a finding about you. Without the
record, it is a feeling about a year you will not remember clearly.

## What to do with the answer

If they do track your cycle, you gain two real things: you stop hunting for a
cause in the food you ate, and you know roughly when the next one is due,
which is the only form of warning this condition offers.

If they do not — which is the likelier result — you have removed the most
plausible-sounding candidate from your list, and what remains is the ordinary
pair of questions: whether yours are [reporting a time or a
place](/blog/why-do-i-keep-getting-canker-sores), and how the one you have now
is [getting on](/blog/how-long-do-canker-sores-last).

Either way, the cycle is on the trigger list in the app for this reason. It is
the entry that needs no judgement call to record and no memory to interpret —
just two dates and a subtraction, kept for three months.
